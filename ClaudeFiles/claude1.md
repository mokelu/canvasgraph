This plan correctly landed on unstorage and got the overall shape right — driver-based, environment-detected, single storage door for the app. But there are a few real bugs and one important architecture question buried in here before you'd want to ship it.

**What's solid:**
- `unstorage/drivers/fs` and `unstorage/drivers/indexeddb` are both real, correctly-named built-in drivers — I checked unstorage's docs to confirm. Good instinct pulling those in rather than hand-rolling.
- `pluginStorage` as a thin namespaced wrapper (`getAll`/`add`/`remove`/`has`) is exactly the shape your machine's `saveToX`/`loadFromX` services were reaching for, just cleaner.
- The environment-detection split (`isDesktop`/`isBrowser`) choosing a driver once, at startup, is the right instinct — this is what collapses your 16-state machine down to "pick a driver, then just call `storage.getItem`."

**Real bugs to fix in the OPFS driver (`opfs.js`):**
- `hasItem`: `const handles = await root.keys()` doesn't give you an array. `root.keys()` returns an *async iterator*, not a promise — awaiting it just hands you back the iterator object, and `.includes()` doesn't exist on that. You need to loop it: `for await (const k of root.keys()) { if (k === key) return true }`, or catch the error from `getFileHandle` instead (simpler — call `getFileHandle(key)` in a try/catch and return `true`/`false`).
- `getKeys`: `[...await root.keys()]` has the same problem — spread (`...`) only works on synchronous iterables, and `root.keys()` is async-only. It'll throw `is not iterable`, not silently misbehave. Needs the same `for await` collection pattern used correctly in `clear()` just below it.

**One risky move — overriding `window.localStorage`:** Assigning a fake object with throwing methods over the real `localStorage` is meant to enforce "always use core/storage," but in most browsers `localStorage` is a non-configurable property on `window`, so this assignment can throw or silently no-op depending on the browser — it's not reliable. It also breaks anything else on the page that legitimately expects `localStorage` to exist (devtools extensions, analytics, third-party widgets). A lint rule or code-review convention gets you the same discipline without touching a global you don't fully control.

**The bigger open question — where does `fsDriver` actually run?** If "desktop" here means Electron with the now-standard `contextIsolation: true` / `nodeIntegration: false` setup (which you want for security), the renderer process can't touch Node's `fs` directly at all — `process.versions?.node` may not even exist there, and `fsDriver` would fail regardless of the `isDesktop` check. In that setup, storage calls from the renderer need to go through a preload script + IPC to the main process, which *then* uses `fsDriver`. Worth confirming: is your desktop shell Electron with context isolation, or something else (Tauri, NW.js, Electron with nodeIntegration on)? That answer changes where `core/storage/index.js` is allowed to live.

**Smaller note, not a blocker:** this plan quietly drops the FS/DB two-column split from a couple messages ago — everything (including the plugins list) now goes through one driver per platform. That's actually a reasonable simplification for now: `unstorage`'s `fs`/`indexedDB` drivers already give you async key-value semantics *on top of* files, so it behaves like your "DB" column even though it's file-backed underneath. You don't need real SQLite until you need actual queries (filtering, joins, indexes) — just flag it to yourself as a deliberate simplification, not something that got lost.
